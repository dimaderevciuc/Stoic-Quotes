# Five example situations (Checkpoint 3b)

Shown in the card order Dima chose: plain explanation first, then the original quote, then how it applies. The full passage opens on tap in the app.

## Rejected after a job interview

Category: work. Matched by phrases like: "didn't get the job after the interview"; "they picked someone else for the position"; "got a rejection email from my dream company".

1. **The obstacle can become the path. A mind that adapts turns what blocks it into a way forward.**
   > for the mind converts and changes every hindrance to its activity into an aid; and so that which is a hindrance is made a furtherance to an act; and that which is an obstacle on the road helps us on this road.
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 5.20 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: This rejection can still move you forward: what you learn from it becomes part of your next application.

2. **Strong people treat failure as part of the path, not the end of it.**
   > those strong ones who only recognize failure as one of the pathways to attainment
   > James Allen, As a Man Thinketh, Thought And Purpose (1903)
   How it applies: One no is a step on the path, not the end of it.

3. **Judge yourself by why and how you act, not only by results you cannot fully control.**
   > And finally, the wise man regards the reason for all his actions, but not the results.
   > Seneca, Letter 14.16 (tr. Richard M. Gummere, 1917)
   How it applies: You controlled your preparation and effort, not their decision. Judge yourself by those.

4. **Do not torment yourself with the fear of being a nobody. You can be somebody in what truly matters: your character.**
   > Let not these thoughts afflict you, I shall live unhonoured and be nobody nowhere.
   > Epictetus, Enchiridion 24 (tr. George Long, 1877)
   How it applies: Your worth is not decided by one hiring panel.

Exercise:
1. Write down three things that went well in the interview and one to improve.
2. If you can, ask the company for brief feedback.
3. Send one new application today, while the lesson is fresh.

## Furious at another driver

Category: anger. Matched by phrases like: "a driver cut me off"; "road rage"; "someone almost crashed into me".

1. **Being angry at people for their faults is like being angry at someone for tripping in the dark. Most mistakes come from not seeing clearly.**
   > what shall we say of one who is angry with those who stumble in the dark
   > Seneca, Of Anger 2.10 (tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
   How it applies: The other driver may simply not have seen you, like someone tripping in the dark.

2. **When you are angry, wait. A little time lets the first heat cool and the fog in your head clear.**
   > delay is the best remedy for it, because it allows its first glow to subside, and gives time for the cloud which darkens the mind either to disperse or at any rate to become less dense
   > Seneca, Of Anger 3.12 (tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
   How it applies: Let the first heat cool before you react.

3. **When someone's fault annoys you, look at your own similar faults. Anger usually fades.**
   > When thou art offended at any man's fault, forthwith turn to thyself and reflect in what like manner thou dost err thyself
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 10.30 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: You have probably made driving mistakes too.

4. **Leave a wrong where it happened. Do not carry it around with you.**
   > Let the wrong which is done by a man stay there where the wrong was done.
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 7.29 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: Leave it on the road. Do not carry it into your day.

Exercise:
1. Breathe out slowly three times before doing anything.
2. Drive on; do not follow or confront.
3. When you arrive, notice if the anger is still worth keeping.

## I keep procrastinating

Category: study. Matched by phrases like: "I keep putting things off"; "I can't make myself start"; "always leaving everything to the last minute".

1. **Starting is the hardest part. Once improvement begins, it starts to feel good.**
   > The mind must, therefore, be forced to make a beginning; from then on, the medicine is not bitter; for just as soon as it is curing us it begins to give pleasure.
   > Seneca, Letter 50.9 (tr. Richard M. Gummere, 1917)
   How it applies: Starting is the hardest part. Once you begin, it gets easier.

2. **We often plan to be better tomorrow instead of being better today. Start now.**
   > thou choosest rather to become good to-morrow than to be good to-day.
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 8.22 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: Instead of planning to start tomorrow, start a small piece today.

3. **Ask yourself honestly what excuses you are making for not working on yourself.**
   > Why are we still indolent and negligent and sluggish, and why do we seek pretences for not labouring and not being watchful in cultivating our reason?
   > Epictetus, Discourses 1.7 (tr. George Long, 1877)
   How it applies: Ask honestly what excuse you are using, then answer it.

4. **Stop waiting for a perfect future. Help yourself now, while you still can.**
   > Hasten then to the end which thou hast before thee, and, throwing away idle hopes, come to thy own aid, if thou carest at all for thyself, while it is in thy power.
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 3.14 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: Stop waiting for the perfect moment. Help yourself now.

Exercise:
1. Pick the smallest possible first step.
2. Set a timer for 10 minutes and do only that step.
3. When the timer ends, decide if you want 10 more.

## My mother or father died

Category: loss, sensitive. Matched by phrases like: "my mother died"; "my dad passed away"; "I lost my father".

1. **Being wise does not mean not grieving. Nobody should expect you to feel nothing after a loss.**
   > as for her not allowing us to grieve at all, that we ought neither to expect nor to wish for
   > Seneca, Consolation to Polybius 18 (tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
   How it applies: Grieving your parent is right. Nobody should expect you to feel nothing.

2. **Time is a real healer. Even the deepest grief usually softens as the months go by.**
   > Even time itself, nature’s greatest remedy, which quiets the most bitter grief
   > Seneca, Consolation to Marcia 1 (tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
   How it applies: The pain is sharpest now. Time will soften it, slowly.

3. **The people who loved us would want to be remembered with warmth, not with endless pain.**
   > He wishes to be fondly remembered by you, but not to be a source of agony to you.
   > Seneca, Consolation to Polybius 5 (tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
   How it applies: Your mother or father would want to be remembered with warmth, not endless pain.

4. **In grief we often pull away from the people closest to us. Let them in; shared sorrow is lighter.**
   > Those who are in sorrow are wont to flee from those who are dearest to them, and to seek liberty for the indulgence of their grief: do you let her share your every thought
   > Seneca, Consolation to Helvia 19 (tr. Aubrey Stewart, 1889)
   How it applies: Let the people close to you share this with you.

Exercise:
1. Let the tears come when they come.
2. Tell one person a favourite memory of your parent.
3. If the sadness feels too heavy to carry, talk to a doctor or call Telefonseelsorge on 142 (Austria). Other countries: findahelpline.com.

## My partner controls, threatens or hurts me

Category: relationships, sensitive. Matched by phrases like: "my partner controls everything I do"; "my boyfriend threatens me"; "my partner checks my phone and won't let me see friends".

1. **Needing help is not a weakness. What matters is that the job gets done, not that you did it alone.**
   > Be not ashamed to be helped; for it is thy business to do thy duty like a soldier in the assault on a town.
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 7.7 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: Needing help is not a weakness. Reaching out is a strong and wise step.

2. **Problems of body and mind are easier to treat early. Do not wait to ask for help.**
   > At present, we do not even consult the physician, whose work would be easier if he were called in when the complaint was in its early stages.
   > Seneca, Letter 50.4 (tr. Richard M. Gummere, 1917)
   How it applies: Do not wait to ask for help. The earlier, the better.

3. **Others may limit your body or circumstances, but your will, your power to choose, stays your own.**
   > You may fetter my leg, but my will not even Zeus himself can overpower.
   > Epictetus, Discourses 1.1 (tr. George Long, 1877)
   How it applies: Someone may try to control you, but your will and your worth stay your own.

4. **Someone who wronged you has not damaged your mind or your character. That part of you is still whole.**
   > the wrong-doer has done thee no harm, for he has not made thy ruling faculty worse than it was before.
   > Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 7.22 (tr. George Long, 1862)
   How it applies: What they do does not make you less. Your character is still whole.

Exercise:
1. If you are in danger right now, call 112. To talk to someone, call Telefonseelsorge on 142 (Austria) or find a helpline at findahelpline.com.
2. Tell someone you trust what is happening.
3. If you can, keep important documents and a phone in a safe place.
